## 1. Global Market Overview

The global market environment for digital medical education and clinical decision support is undergoing significant transformation.

Advances in artificial intelligence, increasing demand for accessible medical knowledge, healthcare workforce challenges, and the digitalization of healthcare systems are changing how medical students and healthcare professionals learn, access information, and make clinical decisions.

This transformation is taking place internationally, although the pace of adoption and specific market needs differ considerably between regions.

### Key Global Market Indicators

**Digital Healthcare Transformation**

According to the World Health Organization (WHO), 129 countries had established national digital health strategies by May 2025.

The WHO also extended its Global Strategy on Digital Health through 2027, demonstrating the continued international importance of healthcare digitalization.

**Healthcare Workforce Demand**

The WHO estimates a global shortage of approximately 11 million healthcare workers by 2030, concentrated largely in low- and lower-middle-income countries.

This creates ongoing pressure to expand medical training capacity, strengthen professional education, and improve the efficiency of healthcare delivery.

**Artificial Intelligence in Medical Education**

A systematic review and meta-analysis published in August 2026 examined 96 studies involving more than 45,000 medical students across 37 countries.

The study found broadly positive attitudes toward AI in medical education, although results varied significantly between countries and institutions.

**Market Outlook**

These developments suggest continued demand for digital solutions that help healthcare professionals:

- Access accurate and current medical knowledge.
- Improve medical learning and examination preparation.
- Integrate AI responsibly into educational and clinical workflows.
- Develop and maintain professional competencies.
- Navigate growing volumes of scientific and clinical information.

For companies operating in digital medical education and clinical knowledge services, the combination of accessible information, educational effectiveness, and clinical reliability is becoming increasingly important.

## 2. Global Market Trends

### Trend 1: Global Expansion of Digital Healthcare and Medical Education

**Market Development**

Healthcare systems worldwide are increasingly incorporating digital technologies into clinical services, medical education, and health information management.

Governments and international health organizations are investing in digital infrastructure, electronic health records, telemedicine, health information exchange, and digital workforce training.

The development extends across both high-income countries and emerging economies.

However, differences in internet access, infrastructure, affordability, and digital literacy continue to influence how quickly these technologies can be adopted.

**Market Implications**

Digital education and medical knowledge platforms can offer advantages such as:

- Flexible access to educational materials.
- Opportunities for remote and self-directed learning.
- More efficient distribution of updated information.
- Support for continuing professional development.
- Greater accessibility for geographically dispersed learners.

At the same time, solutions must account for differences in technical infrastructure and users' digital capabilities.

**Future Outlook**

Digital-first medical learning and knowledge access are likely to remain important components of healthcare transformation.

However, successful adoption will depend on accessibility, affordability, usability, and integration into existing educational and clinical environments.

**Sources:** [1], [4], [5]

### Trend 2: Rapid Adoption of Artificial Intelligence in Healthcare

**Market Development**

Artificial intelligence is increasingly being used to support clinical workflows, medical research, documentation, and information retrieval.

AI applications include:

- Medical research summarization.
- Clinical documentation assistance.
- Medical image interpretation.
- Clinical information retrieval.
- Diagnostic decision support.
- Administrative task automation.

As a regional indicator, the American Medical Association reported that 81% of surveyed US physicians used AI professionally in 2026, compared with 38% in 2023.

Although this figure represents the United States rather than the global physician population, it demonstrates how rapidly AI adoption can develop in established healthcare markets.

International health organizations are also developing frameworks for responsible AI implementation.

**Market Implications**

Healthcare professionals increasingly need to understand how AI systems work, where they can provide value, and what limitations they may have.

Demand for AI-supported medical information tools is accompanied by concerns about:

- Medical accuracy.
- Reliability of AI-generated information.
- Transparency of information sources.
- Patient privacy.
- Clinical accountability.
- Human oversight.

**Future Outlook**

AI is expected to play a growing role in medical information processing and healthcare workflows.

However, evidence-based validation, responsible implementation, and maintaining human clinical judgment will remain essential.

**Sources:** [6], [7]

### Trend 3: Increasing Integration of Generative AI into Medical Education

**Market Development**

Generative AI is changing how medical students interact with educational content.

Students are increasingly exploring AI tools to explain complex medical concepts, summarize information, generate practice questions, and support independent studying.

The 2026 international systematic review of medical students' attitudes toward AI found:

- 96 studies covering 37 countries and more than 45,000 students.
- A pooled estimate of 76.9% reporting positive attitudes toward AI.
- A pooled estimate of 76.6% supporting the integration of AI into medical curricula.

The researchers emphasized that differences between study populations were substantial and that these estimates should not be interpreted as stable global prevalence rates.

**Market Implications**

Medical education is moving toward a broader range of digital and AI-assisted learning experiences.

Relevant developments include:

- Interactive explanations of medical concepts.
- Personalized learning assistance.
- AI-supported examination preparation.
- Practice-based clinical reasoning exercises.
- Training in responsible AI use.

Medical students also need to understand the risks of inaccurate, biased, or misleading AI-generated answers.

**Future Outlook**

AI literacy may become an increasingly important component of medical education.

Educational institutions and digital learning providers will need to balance technological innovation with scientific accuracy and strong educational principles.

**Sources:** [3], [8]

### Trend 4: Growing Importance of Evidence-Based Clinical Information

**Market Development**

Medical knowledge continues to expand through new research, updated clinical guidelines, and advances in diagnostics and treatment.

Healthcare professionals must frequently evaluate information while working under time constraints.

Digital knowledge platforms can help organize medical evidence and make relevant information easier to find.

However, access to large amounts of information does not automatically translate into better clinical decisions.

**Market Implications**

Important characteristics of high-quality digital medical information include:

- Scientific reliability.
- References to relevant evidence.
- Clear organization of medical knowledge.
- Regular content review and updating.
- Transparency regarding uncertainty.
- Contextual relevance to clinical questions.

Requirements can vary by market because clinical guidelines, approved treatments, healthcare resources, and medical practices differ internationally.

**Future Outlook**

Reliable and clearly sourced medical knowledge is likely to remain valuable as generative AI makes it easier to produce large volumes of health-related information.

Differentiating verified medical evidence from unsupported or outdated information will become increasingly important.

**Sources:** [7], [9]

### Trend 5: Personalized Learning and Evidence-Based Study Methods

**Market Development**

Medical education increasingly incorporates digital tools that support individual learning needs and active knowledge retention.

Learning methods such as active recall, self-assessment, and spaced repetition can help students organize and review large amounts of medical information.

A 2026 systematic review and meta-analysis published in *The Clinical Teacher* included 13 studies in its quantitative analysis, covering 21,415 learners.

The researchers found a statistically significant improvement in objective knowledge-test performance associated with spaced repetition compared with standard studying techniques.

However, further research is needed to determine optimal implementation approaches and longer-term outcomes.

**Market Implications**

Digital learning environments can support:

- Personalized revision schedules.
- Practice questions and knowledge assessments.
- Identification of learning gaps.
- Repeated review of difficult concepts.
- Flexible preparation for medical examinations.

Learning needs will differ depending on educational stage, curriculum, and country-specific examination systems.

**Future Outlook**

The combination of evidence-based learning methods and digital personalization offers opportunities to improve educational experiences.

Future developments may include more adaptive learning systems, although their effectiveness must be evaluated rather than assumed.

**Source:** [10]

### Trend 6: Medical Workforce Shortages and Demand for Continuous Education

**Market Development**

Healthcare workforce shortages remain a major international challenge.

According to the WHO, the global healthcare workforce shortfall is projected to reach approximately 11 million people by 2030.

The challenges are particularly significant in regions experiencing limited healthcare investment, population growth, or difficulties retaining qualified professionals.

Workforce challenges differ considerably across countries and may include:

- Insufficient training capacity.
- Shortages of qualified healthcare professionals.
- Unequal geographic distribution.
- Limited employment opportunities for trained workers.
- Migration of healthcare professionals.
- High workloads and professional burnout.

**Market Implications**

Medical education and professional training remain essential for strengthening healthcare systems.

Relevant needs include:

- Expanding access to medical education.
- Supporting the transition from medical school into clinical practice.
- Maintaining professional knowledge throughout medical careers.
- Strengthening clinical competencies.
- Providing flexible opportunities for continuing education.

Digital learning solutions can support training and knowledge access, but they cannot independently resolve structural workforce shortages.

**Future Outlook**

Medical education and continuing professional development will remain important priorities in both developed and emerging healthcare markets.

Solutions that make high-quality education more accessible may be particularly relevant in regions with limited training resources.

**Sources:** [2], [11]

### Trend 7: Increasing Importance of AI Safety, Regulation, and Data Protection

**Market Development**

The growing use of AI in healthcare is creating greater attention to reliability, ethics, transparency, and regulatory compliance.

In 2025, the WHO published guidance on the ethics and governance of large multimodal AI models in healthcare.

UNESCO has also issued international guidance addressing responsible generative AI use in education and research.

Regional regulatory approaches are evolving at different speeds.

For example, the European Health Data Space Regulation entered into force in March 2025, with major provisions scheduled for phased implementation over subsequent years.

**Market Implications**

Healthcare and medical education technology providers must consider:

- Data privacy and information security.
- Appropriate handling of sensitive medical information.
- Regulatory requirements in specific countries.
- Clinical reliability and safety.
- Human responsibility for medical decisions.
- Transparency regarding AI capabilities and limitations.

Regulatory requirements may differ substantially between markets and between educational and clinical applications.

**Future Outlook**

Trust, transparency, and responsible AI implementation will remain important considerations for healthcare technology adoption.

International expansion requires awareness of country-specific legal, medical, and data protection requirements.

**Sources:** [7], [8], [12]

### 3.2 Europe

**Key Market Developments**

European healthcare systems are advancing digitalization while placing significant emphasis on data protection, interoperability, and regulatory oversight.

The European Health Data Space Regulation entered into force on March 26, 2025.

Its implementation follows a phased timeline, with major requirements applying from 2029 onward.

The WHO Regional Office for Europe also identified infrastructure, training, workload, ethical, legal, and technical barriers to the adoption of digital health technologies.

**Market Implications**

Important characteristics of European markets include:

- High importance of data protection.
- Growing interest in digital health interoperability.
- Demand for evidence-based educational and clinical information.
- Different national medical curricula and professional requirements.
- Multilingual communication needs.

**Market Outlook**

Digital healthcare adoption is expected to continue, but implementation will remain influenced by national healthcare systems, technical readiness, and regulation.

#### India: A Relevant Example of an Expanding Medical Education Market

India is particularly relevant when examining the relationship between healthcare workforce development, medical education, and digital transformation.

**Medical Education Expansion**

According to India's Ministry of Health and Family Welfare, 48,563 additional MBBS seats were added between the academic years 2020–21 and 2025–26.

This expansion reflects efforts to increase domestic medical training capacity.

**Digital Healthcare Infrastructure**

India is also developing a large national digital health ecosystem through the Ayushman Bharat Digital Mission (ABDM).

Government reporting in September 2026 stated that more than 976 million Ayushman Bharat Health Account (ABHA) identifiers had been created.

These are cumulative identifiers and should not be interpreted as equivalent to active users of digital medical education products.

**Market Implications**

Potentially relevant market characteristics include:

- An expanding population of medical students.
- Country-specific undergraduate and postgraduate examination requirements.
- Growing national investment in digital healthcare infrastructure.
- Opportunities for digital learning and knowledge access.
- Variation in affordability, language preferences, and connectivity.

**Implications for Medical Education Content**

Content intended for Indian audiences should account for:

- Local medical curricula and examination formats.
- Educational terminology used in India.
- Locally relevant healthcare examples.
- Country-specific clinical guidelines where applicable.
- The needs of medical students at different educational stages.

**Important:** These market developments do not establish that AMBOSS has launched, plans to launch, or offers particular products in India. Such claims require separate company-specific evidence.

### 3.6 Africa

**Key Market Developments**

Healthcare workforce development is one of the most important challenges affecting many African healthcare systems.

The WHO's *State of the Health Workforce in Africa 2026* report revised the projected health workforce shortage in its African Region to approximately 5.85 million by 2030.

The report also highlighted a structural challenge: healthcare systems can face substantial workforce shortages while trained healthcare professionals remain unemployed because of limitations in financing, employment, and workforce distribution.

This demonstrates that expanding education alone is insufficient without corresponding healthcare system investment.

**Market Implications**

Relevant needs include:

- Access to high-quality medical education.
- Continuing professional development.
- Affordable and accessible digital learning.
- Adaptation to different infrastructure conditions.
- Support for healthcare workforce competencies.

Educational resources may need to accommodate differences in connectivity, device availability, language, and curriculum requirements.

**Future Outlook**

Healthcare workforce education and digital health development are likely to remain important priorities.

However, affordability, equitable access, infrastructure, and sustainable financing will influence adoption.
