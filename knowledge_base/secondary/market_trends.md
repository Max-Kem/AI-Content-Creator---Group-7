## 1. Market Overview

The healthcare industry is undergoing significant digital transformation, driven by advances in artificial intelligence, increasing demand for medical education, and the growing complexity of clinical decision-making.

Healthcare professionals and medical students increasingly rely on digital tools to access medical knowledge, prepare for examinations, and support clinical workflows.

Several important market trends are shaping the future of digital medical education and clinical knowledge platforms:

1. Rapid adoption of artificial intelligence in clinical practice.
2. Increasing demand for accessible, evidence-based medical information.
3. Growing use of generative AI in medical education.
4. Greater emphasis on personalized and active learning.
5. Increasing pressure to improve clinical efficiency and reduce physician burnout.
6. Growing demand for medical training and continuous professional development.
7. Increasing importance of AI safety, transparency, and regulatory compliance.

These developments are particularly relevant to AMBOSS, which operates at the intersection of medical education, evidence-based knowledge, and clinical decision support.

## 2. Key Market Trends

### Trend 1: Rapid Adoption of Artificial Intelligence in Clinical Practice

**Market Development**

Artificial intelligence is becoming an increasingly established part of medical practice.

According to the American Medical Association's 2026 Physician AI Survey:

- **81% of surveyed physicians** reported using AI in their practices, compared with 38% in 2023.
- **39%** reported using AI to summarize medical research or standards of care.
- Physicians increasingly recognize the potential of AI to support clinical decisions and reduce administrative workloads.

AI adoption is therefore extending beyond experimentation into everyday professional activities.

**Market Implications**

Healthcare professionals increasingly expect digital tools to deliver relevant information efficiently. AI-powered medical knowledge systems have the potential to reduce time spent searching for information and support clinical workflows.

However, increasing adoption also creates greater demand for reliable and medically validated AI applications.

**Relevance for AMBOSS**

AMBOSS operates in a market where AI-supported medical knowledge retrieval and clinical assistance are becoming increasingly important.

Relevant content topics include:

- Practical applications of AI in medicine.
- AI-supported access to medical knowledge.
- Benefits and limitations of clinical AI.
- Responsible integration of AI into healthcare workflows.

**Source:** [American Medical Association – Physician AI Adoption, March 2026](https://www.ama-assn.org/press-center/ama-press-releases/ama-ai-usage-among-doctors-doubles-confidence-technology-grows)

### Trend 2: Increasing Demand for Evidence-Based Medical Information

**Market Development**

Medical professionals must continuously interpret scientific findings, clinical guidelines, and new treatment recommendations.

Access to information alone is not sufficient. Healthcare professionals also need tools that help them identify relevant and trustworthy evidence.

A 2026 systematic review published in *BMJ Open* examined how medical specialists access information for clinical decision-making.

The analysis found that:

- Digital research databases were among the most frequently used information sources.
- Clinical practice guidelines and point-of-care tools were used less frequently than some standalone information sources.
- Barriers remain to accessing and applying systematically summarized medical evidence.

This highlights an ongoing need to make evidence-based information easier to access and use.

**Market Implications**

The market is not simply moving toward larger collections of medical information. There is also a growing need for efficient access to structured, clinically relevant knowledge.

Important requirements include:

- Reliable scientific references.
- Clearly structured medical information.
- Regularly updated clinical knowledge.
- Efficient information retrieval.
- Practical relevance to clinical questions.

**Relevance for AMBOSS**

Evidence-based knowledge is highly relevant to the core needs of AMBOSS's audience.

Content should emphasize the importance of trustworthy medical information and demonstrate how structured knowledge supports learning and clinical decision-making.

Potential content topics include medical guideline updates, clinical knowledge summaries, and evidence-based educational explanations.

**Source:** [BMJ Open – Information Source Usage in Clinical Decision-Making, 2026](https://bmjopen.bmj.com/content/16/3/e099887)

### Trend 3: Growing Use of Generative AI in Medical Education

**Market Development**

Generative AI is increasingly being incorporated into how medical students study, research, and understand complex medical concepts.

A 2025 multicenter survey of 167 medical students in Ontario, Canada, found that:

- **78.9%** had used generative AI.
- **53.0%** reported using generative AI at least weekly.
- **75.9%** supported introducing generative AI as a formal educational resource or teaching topic.
- **91.6%** recognized the risk of inaccurate AI-generated information.

Although the study involved a relatively small sample, it illustrates both the growing acceptance of AI-assisted learning and students' awareness of its limitations.

**Market Implications**

Medical students are increasingly exploring AI tools for:

- Understanding complex medical concepts.
- Summarizing educational information.
- Supporting examination preparation.
- Reviewing clinical guidelines.
- Exploring diagnostic reasoning.

Educational platforms therefore face growing expectations for AI-assisted learning features.

At the same time, medical accuracy remains essential.

**Relevance for AMBOSS**

AMBOSS serves medical students who are increasingly familiar with generative AI.

Educational content can address how AI can support medical learning while explaining the importance of verification, clinical reasoning, and trusted educational sources.

**Source:** [Journal of Medical Education and Curricular Development – Medical Student AI Survey, 2025](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41181167/)

### Trend 4: Personalized Learning and Active Recall Are Becoming More Important

**Market Development**

Medical education increasingly emphasizes learning approaches that promote long-term knowledge retention rather than passive memorization.

Active recall, spaced repetition, and structured self-assessment are important approaches within this development.

A 2026 systematic review and meta-analysis published in *The Clinical Teacher* evaluated spaced repetition in medical education.

The meta-analysis included 13 studies with a combined sample of 21,415 learners and found a statistically significant improvement in objective knowledge-test performance compared with standard study approaches.

However, the authors noted that more research is needed to determine the optimal implementation of these learning methods.

**Market Implications**

Digital medical education tools can support personalized learning experiences through:

- Practice questions and self-assessment.
- Adaptive learning recommendations.
- Repeated review of important concepts.
- Identification of individual knowledge gaps.
- Personalized examination preparation.

The broader development reflects increasing interest in learning effectiveness, flexibility, and individual progress.

**Relevance for AMBOSS**

Personalized learning is relevant to medical students preparing for demanding examinations and building long-term clinical knowledge.

Content opportunities include:

- Evidence-based study techniques.
- Benefits of active recall.
- Spaced repetition for medical learning.
- Personalized approaches to exam preparation.
- Efficient strategies for identifying knowledge gaps.

**Source:** [The Clinical Teacher – Effectiveness of Spaced Repetition in Medical Education, 2026](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41601436/)

### Trend 5: Growing Pressure to Improve Clinical Efficiency and Reduce Burnout

**Market Development**

Healthcare professionals continue to experience significant workload and administrative pressure.

According to the American Medical Association, **41.9% of surveyed physicians reported at least one symptom of burnout in 2025**, compared with 43.2% in 2024.

Although the figures indicate improvement, burnout remains a substantial concern across medical specialties.

Contributing factors include administrative workloads, staffing challenges, clinical responsibilities, and inefficient digital workflows.

**Market Implications**

There is continued demand for healthcare technologies that:

- Reduce administrative workload.
- Support efficient access to information.
- Simplify repetitive professional tasks.
- Improve clinical workflow efficiency.
- Allow clinicians to focus more attention on patient care.

Technology that demonstrably saves time without compromising medical quality is especially relevant in this environment.

**Relevance for AMBOSS**

Clinical efficiency is an important topic for medical knowledge platforms.

AMBOSS-related content can explore how accessible medical knowledge and well-designed digital tools may help clinicians work more efficiently.

However, claims about time savings or improved patient outcomes should only be made when supported by appropriate evidence.

**Source:** [American Medical Association – Physician Burnout Rates, April 2026](https://www.ama-assn.org/press-center/ama-press-releases/ama-physician-burnout-rates-are-falling-specialty-gaps-remain)

### Trend 6: Growing Need for Medical Education and Continuous Professional Development

**Market Development**

Healthcare systems face ongoing workforce challenges, including staffing shortages, changing population needs, and the requirement for continuous medical training.

The World Health Organization projects a global shortage of approximately **11 million health workers by 2030**, concentrated largely in low- and lower-middle-income countries.

Meanwhile, the Association of American Medical Colleges reported that enrollment in US MD-granting medical schools exceeded **100,000 students for the first time** in the 2025–2026 academic year.

Medical education therefore remains important both for preparing future healthcare professionals and supporting existing clinicians.

**Market Implications**

Healthcare education must increasingly support:

- Medical students entering professional training.
- Residents transitioning into clinical practice.
- Experienced physicians maintaining and expanding their knowledge.
- Healthcare professionals adapting to emerging technologies.
- Lifelong learning throughout medical careers.

Digital education can improve access to learning resources, although access, affordability, and infrastructure remain important limitations.

**Relevance for AMBOSS**

This trend reinforces the relevance of digital medical learning resources across different professional stages.

Potential content themes include:

- Medical school and residency preparation.
- The transition from theoretical learning to clinical practice.
- Continuous medical education.
- Lifelong development of clinical skills.
- Preparing healthcare professionals for future challenges.

**Sources:**

- [World Health Organization – Health Workforce](https://www.who.int/health-topics/health-workforce)
- [AAMC – Medical School Enrollment, December 2025](https://www.aamc.org/news/press-releases/medical-school-enrollment-reaches-100000-students-first-time)

### Trend 7: Increasing Importance of AI Safety, Transparency, and Regulatory Compliance

**Market Development**

As AI becomes more widely used in healthcare, questions regarding accuracy, reliability, transparency, and accountability are becoming increasingly important.

Healthcare applications can have direct implications for patient safety, making responsible implementation essential.

In January 2026, the US Food and Drug Administration published updated guidance clarifying how certain clinical decision support software functions are treated under medical device regulations.

The World Health Organization has also published guidance addressing the ethical development and governance of large multimodal AI models in healthcare.

**Market Implications**

Healthcare AI systems face increasing expectations regarding:

- Medical accuracy and reliability.
- Transparency about limitations.
- Protection of sensitive information.
- Appropriate clinical oversight.
- Responsible use of generated recommendations.
- Compliance with applicable regulatory requirements.

These requirements influence how AI-supported healthcare technologies are developed, evaluated, and communicated.

**Relevance for AMBOSS**

Trust is particularly important for companies providing medical information and clinical decision support.

Relevant content themes include:

- Responsible AI in healthcare.
- Clinical safety and AI limitations.
- The importance of evidence-based AI.
- Human oversight in medical decision-making.
- Transparency and trust in digital healthcare tools.

**Sources:**
