## Brand at a glance

AMBOSS is a medical knowledge and learning platform for medical students, clinicians, educators, and institutions. Its U.S. channels pair rigorous, practical medical knowledge with tools intended to help people learn, prepare, and make informed clinical decisions with confidence.

**Core impression:** knowledgeable, clear, supportive, current, and human.

**Positioning in one line:** Help medical learners and professionals cut through complexity with trusted knowledge and smart tools—so they can move forward with confidence.

## Audiences

Speak to the reader's context rather than treating all medical professionals as one audience:

- **Students and exam candidates:** approachable explanations, study support, and useful USMLE preparation.
- **Clinicians:** efficient access to clinical reference information and support in practice.
- **Educators and institutions:** teaching support and solutions for medical education programs.
- **Physician assistant learners and professionals:** include them where the subject or product is relevant.

Use audience-specific examples and terminology. Do not imply that one resource, feature, or outcome applies equally to every audience.

## Message priorities

1. **Make complex knowledge easier to use.** Lead with the question, decision, or learning need being addressed.
2. **Be precise and trustworthy.** Medical content should be accurate, current, and appropriately qualified.
3. **Build confidence through capability, not hype.** Show how a resource supports learning or clinical work; do not promise certainty or perfect outcomes.
4. **Support people across their journey.** The Instagram profile explicitly speaks to people through medical studies and beyond; reflect that long-term, encouraging relationship.
5. **Connect learning and practice.** The website presents learning, exam preparation, clinical reference, and educator support as related parts of the offer.

## Voice and writing

### Sound like

- **Confident, not grandiose:** use direct statements and concrete benefits.
- **Clear, not simplistic:** explain specialized ideas in plain language without sacrificing medical precision.
- **Encouraging, not patronizing:** recognize the workload and uncertainty of medical training and practice.
- **Evidence-minded, not sensational:** support factual and comparative claims with a credible source and context.
- **Current, not breathless:** distinguish genuinely updated information from promotional urgency.

### Writing habits

- Use active voice, short paragraphs, descriptive headings, and scannable lists.
- Put the useful point first; explain details and caveats after it.
- Prefer specific language such as “review,” “practice,” “find,” and “compare” over vague promises.
- Use U.S. English for U.S. channels.
- Write **AMBOSS** in uppercase. Preserve official product and feature names (for example, **AMBOSS AI** and **AI Mode**) as presented in approved product materials.
- Use a clear next step when relevant; website-style calls to action are concise and action-oriented.

### Avoid

- Fear-based exam or patient-care messaging.
- Claims that AMBOSS guarantees a score, diagnosis, treatment decision, or patient outcome.
- Unsupported superlatives, competitor comparisons, or claims of clinical superiority.
- Overuse of jargon, unexplained acronyms, clickbait, and excessive exclamation marks.
- Treating educational or reference content as a substitute for professional judgment.

## Visual direction

The public channels show a restrained, clean identity: the AMBOSS wordmark and geometric mark, teal/turquoise accents, dark teal, white space, and dark neutral text. Instagram profile highlights use simple line illustrations on pale blue backgrounds. The website uses prominent, high-contrast headlines and clear action buttons.

Use these observations as direction, **not as a substitute for official specifications**:

- Use only approved AMBOSS logos and product assets. Do not redraw, recolor, stretch, or add effects to the mark.
- Favor uncluttered layouts, strong hierarchy, generous breathing room, and one clear focal point.
- Use teal as an accent rather than assuming a particular shade or using it for long blocks of small text.
- Keep diagrams and icons simple, purposeful, and medically legible. Avoid decorative clinical imagery that could be mistaken for a real patient or finding.
- Use authentic, respectful depictions of students and healthcare professionals. Represent people and roles inclusively; do not use identifiable patient information.
- On Instagram, design for mobile readability and a strong first-frame or cover hook. Keep text within safe areas and make carousel slides useful individually as well as in sequence.
- Do not treat Instagram's interface elements (such as its gradient story ring) as AMBOSS brand colors.

Exact color codes, font families, logo clear space, and approved image treatments were not established from the reviewed public pages. Retrieve these from AMBOSS's official design assets before production use.

## Channel guidance

### Website

- Lead with a clear benefit and audience-relevant context.
- Structure product information around what the user can do: learn, practice, reference, or teach.
- Give evidence, qualifications, and limitations room alongside performance or research claims.
- Make the call to action specific and easy to understand.

### Instagram (@amboss_med)

- Open with a useful, relatable hook for medical learners or professionals.
- Prioritize bite-sized medical knowledge, study and USMLE tips, timely updates, and community-oriented content, consistent with the profile description.
- Use captions to add context, explain terminology, or qualify a clinical statement—not merely repeat artwork.
- Keep educational content accurate when compressed into a short format; link to a fuller explanation when needed.
- Maintain a supportive and conversational tone while preserving professional credibility.

## Medical accuracy, trust, and safety

- Have qualified reviewers validate medical content, references, and clinical wording before publication.
- Check that evidence and guidance are current; date or qualify content when recommendations may change.
- Cite sources for clinical, scientific, statistical, or comparative claims, and preserve enough context to avoid misleading summaries.
- Clearly distinguish educational information, product descriptions, and clinical guidance. Do not create individualized diagnosis or treatment advice in social content.
- Describe AI capabilities and research findings precisely, including the scope and limitations of the evidence. Do not imply that AI replaces clinician judgment.
- Protect patient privacy and obtain appropriate permissions for testimonials, images, and case examples.
