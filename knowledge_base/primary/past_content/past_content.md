1. LiSA ranks #1 in independent clinical AI evaluation
𝐀𝐌𝐁𝐎𝐒𝐒 𝐋𝐢𝐒𝐀 𝐫𝐚𝐧𝐤𝐬 #1 𝐢𝐧 𝐢𝐧𝐝𝐞𝐩𝐞𝐧𝐝𝐞𝐧𝐭 𝐞𝐯𝐚𝐥𝐮𝐚𝐭𝐢𝐨𝐧 𝐨𝐟 𝐜𝐥𝐢𝐧𝐢𝐜𝐚𝐥 𝐀𝐈 𝐬𝐲𝐬𝐭𝐞𝐦𝐬
A new benchmark study from Stanford, Harvard, and collaborating institutions has just been published, evaluating how 31 AI systems behave in 100 real clinical scenarios drawn from primary-care-to-specialist consultations. We’re excited to share that LiSA, AMBOSS’s AI search agent for clinical care, ranked #1 overall and placed in the top tier in terms of safety.

The NOHARM benchmark is one of the most clinically grounded evaluations to date, using real cases reviewed by subspecialists across 10 specialties and based on 12,747 expert annotations. LiSA was one of the few systems that demonstrated consistently strong performance across all evaluated dimensions: safety, clinical appropriateness, and contextual relevance.

LiSA’s results reflect intentional design choices: she draws exclusively from curated clinical sources (AMBOSS Knowledge Library, selected US guidelines, and drug databases), is developed by physicians and engineers together, and is structured to align with clinical workflows.

For clinicians and healthcare leaders exploring clinical AI solutions, independent validation matters. We welcome the transparency this study brings to the field and are committed to continuing rigorous, responsible development of LiSA.

4. Stanford–Harvard Clinical AI study: LiSA leads
The Stanford-Harvard Clinical AI study is out, and the signal is clear: clinical AI beat the generalist models. And AMBOSS LiSA led the group of purpose-built AI for medicine, with top safety-weighted performance and the fewest severe errors of any system tested.

We made this happen through the efforts of hundreds of full-time physicians and engineers working side by side every day. And as clinicians ourselves, we know that in the real world it was never about who scores the lowest personal error rate. It's about the well-being of each individual patient, and the trust of the peers relying on our tools around the world. While much of the conversation around AI is viral and loud, we've been quietly working towards this one goal. And it looks like we succeeded.

Our thanks to ARISE for leading this important work and raising the standard for clinical AI evaluation once more.
